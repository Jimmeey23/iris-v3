# Film | When to use | Stop or exception | Completion evidence
27 | A bike or microphone check is due. In the default schedule, day five and day ten are counted from resolution of the original, not from the last check. The check goes to the original reporter; the repair owner handles a returned fault. | If you cannot identify the original unit, reproduce the test conditions, or access the outcome controls, do not record a pass. Arrange verification with the repair owner. | Returned fault: the original number reopens with the observation and pending checks in that cycle are cancelled. Passed check: No recurrence appears against this day; a later scheduled check is not marked passed.
28 | You know the affected studio and can describe one failure without the assistant interpreting a chat report. | Stop at review if the title, route or studio disagrees with the report. If submission appears slow, look for the saved record before trying again. | The newly created ticket can be opened by number and shows the same title after reload.
29 | A free-text incident needs classification and structured equipment details. | If the proposed route conflicts with the symptom, correct both category and subtype. If a required fact is unknown, confirm it instead of guessing merely to reach approval. | Approval returns a ticket number. Opening and reloading that record reproduces the approved incident.
30 | Another report mentions equipment that already has an open repair. | Matching equipment is not enough: compare studio and symptom. A parts request is not the same work as repairing the unit. | Either the existing repair carries the new observation, or the differences justify a distinct incident.
31 | The shift lead needs urgent open work with no current owner. | An empty intersection is a valid result. Check the scope and remove one filter before drawing a wider conclusion. | For a candidate requiring handover, the exact ticket and its actual ownership have been checked.
32 | A lead needs to repeat the same priority review on another shift. | If the recalled conditions differ, do not use the row count as a comparison. Verify the filter indicators first. | Clearing the queue and reopening the saved view restores the intended priority filter.
33 | Two studios appear to have different repair backlogs. | Do not compare one studio's open-only count with another studio's all-status count. Collapsing a section does not remove its tickets. | The comparison names the scope, grouping level and priority slice used for both studios.
34 | Someone remembers the reporting day but cannot supply the ticket number. | Multiple reports can share a date and a similar title. Verify the studio and equipment before quoting an event. | The cited event includes the matched ticket, actor and event time, not just the report date.
35 | Work appears to have stopped moving across status columns. | Do not move a card merely because it has been in the same column for a long time. Read the dependency first. | The next check is documented as a follow-up or the existing reason for waiting is understood.
36 | Repair work has started but the owner needs a response to proceed. | A parts delay and a missing instructor confirmation are different blockers. Do not choose Awaiting Response unless someone actually owes a reply. | Status and the saved log entry agree on what is pending and who should respond.
37 | The owner believes the fix is ready to close. | A spare keeping a class running is a workaround, not proof the original unit is fixed. Do not claim a cause that has not been verified. | The resolution describes the test and its result, and the saved status reads Resolved.
38 | An incoming shift must perform a specific equipment test. | Do not assume that entering a person's name in the log reassigns the ticket. Check ownership separately. | A saved Follow-up names the shift, unit and observable next action.
39 | The reporter disputes whether the first response was timely. | If the history does not support a conclusion, report the displayed milestone and the missing evidence separately. | The answer distinguishes first response from resolution and cites the relevant event.
40 | The repair owner cannot authorize a required decision. | Do not escalate to compensate for a vague note. Identify the exact approval, part or intervention needed. | History shows the intended new level and the recorded decision request.
41 | A higher-level blocker is cleared and the local owner can continue. | Do not lower the rung solely because time passed; verify the approval or other change that removes the blocker. | History records why the level decreased, without implying that the repair itself was completed.
42 | A colleague questions why or when a ticket moved up the ladder. | A timer predicts a move. It does not replace a historical event. Keep current level and prior reasons separate. | The explanation names the actual move, its reason and the current rung.
43 | A repair depends on a separate supply or parts work order. | A similar title is not proof of a dependency. Check the destination before relying on the link. | The original shows a linked number that opens the intended separate work order.
44 | The front desk has useful information and another shift may need to act. | Do not treat a comment as a resolution, ownership change or scheduled test. Record an actionable follow-up separately. | The thread shows the factual update; the log contains any specific work still owed.
45 | A ticket needs a descriptive label shared across several kinds of work. | If the department or classification is wrong, a tag alone will not fix it. Treat intake routing as a separate decision. | Details shows the intended tag without changing the meaning of the routing category.
46 | A known ticket is absent from the current queue. | If the number still does not appear in All Tickets, check the number and your access with the appropriate lead; do not raise a duplicate on that basis. | The opened record matches the original number and title, or the visibility question is passed on explicitly.
47 | A colleague hands over a specific repair number during a call. | If the result has the wrong number, stop before discussing details or recording an update. | Search result, ticket header, affected studio and owner all refer to the intended case.
48 | Similar titles make it easy to put a note on the wrong record. | Distinguish an equipment incident from procurement work, even when both mention the same unit. | The selected workspace describes the task the new evidence actually belongs to.
49 | A due bike follow-up is ready for an observed test. | No complaint is not a successful test. If the bike cannot be checked, keep the outcome pending. | The check shows No recurrence and the original schedule distinguishes this pass from later checks.
50 | A microphone exhibits the repaired fault again during a recurrence check. | If the symptom or equipment differs, establish the relationship before reopening the original. Record observations rather than an assumed diagnosis. | The original repair number is reopened and the recurrence note states the unit, trigger and repeated symptom.
51 | A lead needs to brief another shift on open or escalated work. | Do not invent an owner, decision or return time. If one is missing, say exactly what still needs confirmation. | The incoming lead can identify the ticket, current responsibility, next action and any outstanding approval.
